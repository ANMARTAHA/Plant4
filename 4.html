The essentials, at a glance

No long lecture here — just the rules and facts worth memorizing before an exam.

What is a saponin?

A glycoside from higher plants. Hydrolysis splits it into a sugar (glycon) and a nonpolar aglycon called a sapogenin.

Two defining properties

  • Persistent foaming in water (emulsifier)
  • Hemolysis of red blood cells

The golden safety rule

Extremely toxic IV (destroys RBC membranes) but harmless orally — high molecular weight & polarity block GIT absorption.

Chemical split

  • Steroidal (C25) — neutral, spiroketal side chain
  • Triterpenoid (C30) — acidic, more common in nature

Shared architecture

Both types link sugar at position 3 and both arise biosynthetically from mevalonic acid / isoprenoid units.

Sapogenins ≠ drugs

Not therapeutic themselves — but key starting material for steroidal hormones (cortisone, oral contraceptives).

Applications

  • Detergents & emulsifiers
  • Beverage foaming agents
  • Fish poisons (cold-blooded toxicity)
  • Expectorants (Ipecac, liquorice)

Three signature drugs

  • Glycyrrhiza — glycyrrhizin, 50× sweeter than sugar
  • Fenugreek — diosgenin source, antidiabetic
  • Ginseng — ginsenoside, sexual stimulant/adaptogen
C25 vs C30

Steroidal = neutral, spirostan skeleton, Diosgenin is the industrial leader. Triterpenoid = acidic, Dammarane (ginsenoside) or Oleanane (amyrins) family.
